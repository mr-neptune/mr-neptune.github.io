Noise to Music

A 16-bar song of random notes. An optimizer nudges them, one step at a time, until they sound like the genre you pick. Press play and listen to it learn.

How it works

The model. A 16-bar song in A A B A′ form: a 4-bar section A, a contrasting 4-bar section B, and a final bar that closes the last A. Each bar has a melody, a counter-melody, a bass line and chords on a 16th-note grid. At step 0 every pitch, rhythm and chord is random.

The critic. A style model per genre, trained offline on about 2,400 real MIDI songs (roughly 270,000 bars). From each song it extracted the melody, the bass line and a chord for every half bar. It then learned how melodies move, which chord tones they land on at each beat strength, which rhythms fill a bar, how chords progress and how often they change, and how the bass relates to the harmony.

The loss. Each section is scored by how unlikely it is under the genre's model (negative log-likelihood), plus how far its statistics drift from the genre's, so it can't win by repeating one safe note. Each term is measured against held-out real music: a term reaches zero once a section is as plausible as the best 10% of real 4-bar phrases. A blend mixes the two models' probabilities.

Form and counterpoint. On top of that, the song is scored as a whole: the chords have to flow from A into B and back, B has to differ from A in tune and harmony, and the song has to end on the home chord. The counter-melody follows classic counterpoint rules: stay under the melody, avoid clashing with it on strong beats, avoid parallel fifths and octaves, and move when the melody holds still.

The optimizer. A genetic algorithm with a population of 200 songs. Each step mutates pitches, rhythms and chords, moves whole bars to a new chord, recombines bars from good parents and keeps what lowers the loss. It descends the loss like training does, only without gradients. Switch genre mid-run and it fine-tunes from where it is.

What is learned. The notes and chords. Instruments, the comping rhythm and the backing drums are genre presets. Everything runs in your browser, and nothing is sent anywhere.

Training data: Lakh MIDI Dataset (Colin Raffel, CC BY 4.0) with tagtraum genre annotations (Hendrik Schreiber), and artist-labelled songs from its clean_midi subset. There is no lo-fi MIDI dataset, so Lo-fi is learned from jazz and R&B under 95 BPM. The classical model is smaller (about 100 pieces) and noisier, because most of those files are human performances rather than quantized scores.
